It's 1840. You need your leg cut off. And there's no anaesthetic. [annoyed] Your only hope... is a fast surgeon. [sighs] But let's go back. Seven thousand years ago, people drilled holes in each other's skulls. And many of them survived. The bone grew back. In the Middle Ages, your surgeon was also your barber. Haircut, shave, bit of bloodletting. [grumbling] By the 1840s, surgeons were timed. Robert Liston, in London, could reportedly take off a leg in under thirty seconds. Speed was the only painkiller. Then, in 1846, a dentist in Boston put a patient to sleep with ether. And the surgeon said: "Gentlemen, this is no humbug." [annoyed] Problem solved? No. Surgeons still operated in bloody coats, with dirty hands. Nearly half of Joseph Lister's amputation patients died. Then he started killing germs with carbolic acid. And his deaths dropped to about one in seven. [sighs] Rubber gloves? Made around 1890, because a nurse's hands were sore from the chemicals. Her surgeon later married her. [annoyed] So next time you moan about the hospital food... remember, in 1840, you'd have been awake for this—
